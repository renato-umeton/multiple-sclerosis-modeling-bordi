These files hold synthetic data, not the clinical series of Bordi et al. 2013.
No clinical claim may be read off them. That first sentence stands on the first
line by itself because msrelapse.datasets copies that line, and only that line,
onto every frame it hands out.

What they are
=============
Seventy simulated relapsing-remitting records, in the three schemas of
msrelapse.io: synthetic_bordi2013_weekly.csv (one row per patient-week),
synthetic_bordi2013_durations.csv (the run length encoding of the same records)
and synthetic_bordi2013_events.csv (one row per relapse). The three describe the
same records and convert into one another, with the caveat that the events to
weekly direction applies the rounding rule of the study and is lossy.

How they were made
==================
Generated by msrelapse version 0.1.0 with the alternating renewal engine and the
fixed seed 20130910, which opens with 2013, the year of the volume the article
appeared in, and closes with four arbitrary digits so that the number is
memorable. The article carries no issue date and none is claimed here. In code:

    from msrelapse import cohort, datasets
    datasets.regenerate_synthetic_bordi2013()          # rewrites these files
    cohort.generate(cohort.bordi2013_spec("renewal"), rng=20130910)

The cohort size, the follow up windows and the two mean durations all come from
the article. Each record is as long as one draw from the histogram of
relapsing-remitting phase lengths of Figure 3, which spans 40 to 1311 weeks.

The generative mean durations are 134.21 weeks in health (remission) and
4.34415 weeks in no health (relapse). They are not the durations the article
prints, and the difference is deliberate. The article reports a mean remission
of about 100 weeks and a mean relapse of 4.3 weeks, and it computes both as
naive means: every recorded run of a state is averaged, including the last
remission of a record, which the end of follow up cut short. Measured that way
over the follow up windows of Figure 3, a cohort generated at 100 weeks reports
about 80. The generative means above were therefore chosen, by
msrelapse.cohort.naive_mean_targets, so that the same naive measurement of this
cohort returns the printed 100 and 4.3 weeks. On these seventy records the naive
means measure 104.3 and 4.12 weeks; the remaining gap is the sampling noise of a
cohort of that size.

What seventy records can pin down
=================================
Seventy records of these lengths hold about 260 relapses and about 260
remissions, so a mean duration measured on them carries a standard error of
about six percent: generate the twin under another seed and the two means move
by that much. Everything built on those means carries that error. The barrier
ratio of equation (7), which the closing table of msrelapse.datasets reports, is
the ratio of the logarithms of the two means, and one standard error of the
means moves it by about 0.13. The table therefore compares it under a tolerance
of 0.2. A tighter comparison, such as the 0.05 that the article's own rounding
of 3.157 to 3.1 would already fail, needs a cohort several times this size and
not a seed chosen to meet it. Nothing about the size of this twin is a choice:
it is seventy records because the study followed seventy patients.

The same holds for the table as a whole, and it is worth knowing before reading
one. Regenerating this twin under the sixty consecutive seeds from 20130910 and
building the closing table on each of the sixty cohorts, every judged row comes
out True on 27 of them, a little under half. The five percent rule on the mean
relapse duration is the one that fails most often, on 24 of the 60, because five
percent is narrower than one standard error of that mean on this many records.
A False row under another seed is the sampling noise of seventy records and not
a fault of the pipeline that built them.

Why a synthetic twin at all
===========================
The article released no data. It carries no data availability statement, no
supplementary material and no deposited series, and the per patient records
behind its Figures 2, 3 and 4 were never published. Anyone who needs the real
series should write to the corresponding authors of the article, Marco Salvetti
and Alfonso Sutera, and ask for it. This package ships a twin so that its
pipeline can be run end to end without it.
