# Design decisions

This page records the decisions taken while building version 0.1 of msrelapse
and the reason for each, so that a reader who wonders why the package does
something one way rather than another finds the answer here. It carries over
the record kept by the stage tracking document the repository used while the
work was in progress. That document was removed once every stage was complete,
because the decisions it held are permanent and the progress it tracked is not.

## Paper and model

**The printed mean durations are naive means.** The article averaged every
recorded run of a state over its own follow up windows, the censored final
remission of each record included. A cohort generated at a true mean of 100
weeks shows a naive mean near 80 weeks over the Figure 3 windows, 79.7 measured
over 20000 windows, so the shipped twin is generated from generative means
chosen so that its naive means reproduce the printed 100 and 4.3 weeks.
`msrelapse.cohort.naive_mean_targets` inverts the measurement, and
`msrelapse.cohort.bordi2013_spec` applies it when `match_naive_means` is true.

**Time varying parameters are out of scope for version 0.1** and are recorded
as future work. The asymmetry and the noise of the article are constants of a
patient. A slowly varying asymmetry or noise, which would let a barrier drift
over a follow up, would need a calibration procedure and a validation the
article gives no material for. Nothing in the equations forbids it, so it is a
later version rather than a closed question.

## Data and provenance

**The package ships a synthetic twin rather than the clinical series.** The
de-identified weekly series cannot be released without a decision from the
co-authors, so the package ships a twin generated by the renewal engine from
mean durations solved so that the naive means reproduce the printed pair, with
loud provenance, and [Data](data.md) says how to request the original. The twin
uses no potential and no fitted asymmetry or noise; those belong to the `sde`
engine, which the shipped files were not generated with.

**The data files ship inside the package**, under `src/msrelapse/data`, with a
loader in `msrelapse.datasets`, so they are available after a `pip install`.
The separate top level data directory of the original specification is not
used.

**Provenance travels beside the records rather than inside them.** The shipped
files have a sidecar `PROVENANCE.txt`, which `msrelapse.datasets.provenance`
returns whole, and `msrelapse.datasets.load_synthetic_bordi2013` copies its
first line onto the frame it returns, as `frame.attrs["provenance"]`. It is not
a column, because the three schemas of `msrelapse.io` reject an extra column as
firmly as a missing one, and it is not a comment row at the head of each CSV,
because that would change the reader contract: every reader of those files,
this package and any other, would have to be told about a comment character
before it could read a shipped file at all.

**Two signatures of the original specification were changed.**
`msrelapse.renewal.alternating_renewal` returns one events frame keyed by
`patient_id` rather than the list of frames the specification declares, because
that single frame is the events schema `msrelapse.io` validates and converts.
`week_length_days` lives on `msrelapse.io.read_events`, where the calendar
dates are parsed, rather than on `msrelapse.io.events_to_weekly`, because by
then the frame holds weeks and there is no day left to divide.

## Simulation and calibration

**The weekly rounding rule marks every week a relapse touches**, so the
continuous time engines are aimed one week below the relapse target and one
week above the health target. `msrelapse.cohort.continuous_targets` returns
that pair, 101 and 3.3 weeks for the published durations. Remissions shorter
than a week merge the relapses on either side of them; that residual is
documented rather than hidden.

**Crossings read off the integration grid overestimate episode lengths** by
about a constant times the square root of the step.
`msrelapse.simulate.exit_times` and the state machine both move the thresholds
by the Brownian bridge shift `BRIDGE_CONSTANT * sigma * sqrt(dt)`, with
`msrelapse.simulate.BRIDGE_CONSTANT` of 0.5826.

**The hysteresis band fraction has two defaults on purpose:**
`msrelapse.model.DEFAULT_BAND_FRACTION` of 0.3, which every band taking
function of `msrelapse.model` and `msrelapse.simulate` uses, and
`msrelapse.cohort.SDE_ENGINE_BAND_FRACTION` of 0.4, which the `sde` engine of
`CohortSpec` uses. The reason is measured rather than stylistic. A band decides
how many complete remissions of a path are shorter than a week, and a remission
shorter than a week merges the two relapses around it once the path is written
as weeks. On the rounding corrected 101 and 3.3 weeks that the measured table
of `CohortSpec` was built on, about one complete remission in five and a half
is shorter than a week at 0.3 and about one in ten at 0.4, which puts the naive
means of an `sde` cohort about a fifth to a third above the renewal ones at 0.3
and about a tenth to a fifth above them at 0.4. It is not widened further: at
0.5 the cohort of the article has no calibration at all, because the asymmetry
a wider band asks for passes the saddle-node fold at `fold_beta(1) = 0.3849`.
The table of measurements is in the Notes of
[`msrelapse.cohort.CohortSpec`](api/cohort.md), and the cost of the wider
default, a pair of durations that calibrates at 0.3 and raises at 0.4, is the
worked example beside it.

## Statistics and tolerances

**The closing table carries one barrier ratio row, the cohort one.** The per
patient ratios of patients 23, 32 and 53 are deliberately not rows of
`msrelapse.datasets.reproduction_table`, because they are read off the
durations the article prints rather than off any record, while the table
measures a weekly frame. They live in `msrelapse.cohort.paper_patients`
instead, one row per patient, with each printed value beside the value
recomputed from its neighbours.

## Packaging and tooling

**Python 3.10 through 3.14 are supported**, with local development on 3.14.

**The command line uses argparse**, which keeps the dependency set minimal.

**The package is named msrelapse**, a name checked as free on PyPI before the
scaffold was written.

**The reproduce command performs the analysis itself** rather than executing
notebook 01, so that the command carries no notebook dependency. The command
and the notebook build the closing table through the one
`msrelapse.datasets.reproduction_table`, so the two agree by construction
rather than by copying. The notebooks are executed with nbclient in
`tests/test_notebooks.py` rather than with papermill, which is why papermill is
not a dependency.

**The first release is 0.1.0 and not the 1.0.0 the original specification names
throughout.** The package is a first public reference implementation with the
interface still open to change, which is what the
`Development Status :: 3 - Alpha` classifier says, and semantic versioning
reserves 1.0.0 for an interface the project undertakes to keep.

**Publishing is left to the maintainer**, because PyPI and Zenodo need account
credentials. The repository prepares the release workflow and the steps in
[Contributing](CONTRIBUTING.md) and stops there.

**The ORCID and the archive DOI are left out of `CITATION.cff` and
`codemeta.json`** rather than filled with a placeholder, because an invalid
ORCID fails citation file validation and an empty identifier misleads a
harvester; each file carries a comment saying where the value goes. The one
literal placeholder that ships is the Zenodo DOI in the software BibTeX entry
of `src/msrelapse/_citation.py`, with a matching commented out badge in
`README.md`, both named by the release steps of
[Contributing](CONTRIBUTING.md).

**`date-released` in `CITATION.cff` is the planned first release date** rather
than a date anything was released on, and it moves with the release if that
date moves. The `comment` key of `codemeta.json` is kept as the maintainer note
it is, saying which identifier fields Zenodo fills in at that release and where
the ORCID belongs.

## Documentation and repository rules

**Markdown lives under `docs/`**, with `README.md` at the repository root the
one exception, so the changelog, the contributing guide, the data provenance
note and the journal draft are all pages of this site or files beside them.

**The stage tracking document is removed once every stage is complete**, which
is why this page exists. The specification the work was built from is a local
file that the repository does not track, so a decision recorded only there
would be lost to a fresh checkout.

**Two items of the closing checklist are deferred to the maintainer**, because
they need people rather than code: a read of [Theory](theory.md) by one
co-author before the tag, and sending the repository link to the co-authors and
the three outreach targets once the archive DOI resolves.

## Stage outcomes

The work ran in five stages. What each of them settled is below.

### Stage 1: the article and the ecosystem

The potential of the article is $V(x) = -x^2/2 + \alpha x^4/4 + \beta x$,
equations (2) and (3), with drift $x - \alpha x^3 - \beta$, noise variance
$\epsilon$, a symmetric barrier of $1/(4\alpha)$ that grows as $\alpha$ falls,
and the health well at negative $x$. The provisional form the original
specification carried was the other parameterisation and was dropped. The
verified transcription of the article is [Paper facts](paper_facts.md).
Calibrating at $\alpha = 1$ to 100 and 4.3 weeks, read as mean first passage
times to the saddle, gives $\beta = 0.210435$ and $\sigma = 0.507768$. The
Kramers form cannot reach 4.3 weeks at all, because its prefactor floor is 4.44
weeks.

### Stage 2: the scaffold

mypy targets Python 3.12, because the numpy stubs use the `type` statement,
which mypy parses only from that version on; the 3.10 floor of the package is
held by the ruff target version instead. ruff is pointed away from `docs/`,
which holds prose and no Python module, so that a script added there later is
left out on purpose rather than by oversight. The MIT classifier is omitted
from `pyproject.toml`, because PyPI rejects an upload that carries both a
license expression and the legacy classifier.

### Stage 3: the modules

All modules, the command line, the public API with `cite()`, a doctest harness
over every module, and the shipped synthetic twin are in place. The periodicity
test reads the relapse onsets, whose spectrum is flat under the memoryless
null, rather than the state series, whose spectrum is red. The barrier ratio
row of the closing table is judged at an absolute tolerance of 0.2, because
seventy records carry sampling noise in the two naive means the ratio is built
from; the 0.05 the original specification asked for could be met on a cohort of
that size only by choosing a seed. The deterministic recomputation from the
printed durations is pinned far more tightly than that, to nine decimals, in
`tests/test_model.py`.

### Stage 4: notebooks, documentation, metadata and data

Four notebooks built by `notebooks/build_notebooks.py` and executed in the
tests; this site, with the theory, reproducing, data, citing, API and paper
facts pages; `CITATION.cff`, `codemeta.json`, the issue forms, the release
workflow, the documentation deploy workflow, and the journal draft under
`docs/paper`.

### Stage 5: reviews and release preparation

Three independent review rounds over the whole implementation, each followed by
fixes and re-verification. Round one raised 117 findings, 24 of them confirmed
as blocking; round two raised 83, 22 of them confirmed as blocking; round three
was a sign off, which raised 62 findings and no critical one. Every confirmed
blocking finding was fixed before the round closed.
